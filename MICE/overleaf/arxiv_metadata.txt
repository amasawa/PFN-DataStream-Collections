Title: Recall or Relearn? A Mixture of In-Context Experts for Recurring Concept Drift
Authors: Zhangkai Wu, Xu Zhang, Zhihong Cui, Kaize Shi
Primary category: cs.LG    Cross-lists: stat.ML
Comments: 37 pages
License: (to choose at submission; arXiv non-exclusive licence is the usual default)

Abstract:
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) learn a stream through their context, so a past concept can be recalled by putting its rows back into the context, without retraining. Under recurring concept drift, when a stored context holds one concept and the experts are separated by a margin, the gain of recall over relearning is the area between the stored context's accuracy and the learning curve, up to a first-batch term. Stored contexts are segments starting at changes detected by a two-sample test on the window's errors, with false-alarm and detection guarantees. We propose MICE, a mixture of window and stored-context experts: a fast meta expert identifies a recurring concept within one batch under a margin; a slow one bounds the discounted half-Brier regret against the plain window by 2 ln 2 = 1.39 at every time. On a controlled grid, learning-curve predictions rank the observed gains with Spearman correlations of 0.94 and 0.83. On 72 fresh grid streams with random boundary phases, MICE exceeds reset detection by 2.85 points on average, on 70 of them; its stored contexts were majority-pure at every close, but 88% held at least 95% rows of one concept, short of the registered 90%, so these gains come from mixed contexts. On 29 real streams (backbone seed 0, fixed segments; detected ones differ by 0.01 points on average) MICE is more accurate than all four in-context baselines on 23, and its one-sided 95% block-bootstrap bound on the difference to the plain window is above -0.3 points on 28. With detected segments, MICE takes 1.07 s per batch on three real prefixes, against 2.58 s for an archive of twelve experts with the same segments; on fresh, shifted and misaligned grid streams it is 5.7 to 6.6 points more accurate than a snapshot pool of two experts and 1.3 to 3.4 points more accurate than one of twelve, which takes 1.3 to 2.6 times as long, except on 2000-row blocks, where they are level.
