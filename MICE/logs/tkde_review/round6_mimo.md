## Verified, no finding
1. **Order and causality.** `MiceV1.step` scores `self.pending` (predictions of batch t-1) first, then closes segments / merges, then predicts batch t (run.py:198–222); `simulate2` uses `prev` (losses of batch t-1) and `Lo` (updated from `last`, the losses of batch t-1) only after those labels exist (reweight.py:121–127, 137). Matches "score → update contexts → predict" and "no expert is scored on rows it already holds".
2. **New/merged experts.** New expert: `simulate2` line 121 uses `np.median(list(prev.values()))` for a key absent from `prev` = the median of the losses of the experts that predicted the scored batch = the algorithm's "median of the ℓ_k". Merge keeps `uid` (run.py:142–144) so the loss record continues.
3. **First step / warm-up.** `prev is None → w = ones`, `Lo = 0`, `last = None` → uniform fast and slow weights (reweight.py:117–127); the run loop starts at `t=1` (`t0_=1`, run.py:278, 284), batch 0 only feeds the contexts. Matches "At t=1 the fast weights are uniform and L_{0,k}=0" and the caption.
4. **Slow level.** `hb(Q) = 0.5·mean‖p−e_y‖²` ∈ [0,1] is exactly the mean half Brier score; `last = scale·[hb(dflt), hb(inner)]`, `default=-1000` (= the M-window), `Lo = g2·Lo + last` with `g2=0.9`, `a ∝ exp(-e2·g2·Lo)` (`temper=True`) (reweight.py:104, 124–127, 132–134). With `scale=0.5, e2=1` the weights equal the paper's `exp(-η2 γ L)` on the *unscaled* half Brier with **η2 = e2·scale = 0.5** (algebraically identical: `exp(-1·γ·0.5·L_hb)`). Discount, indices (`L_{t-1,k}=γ L_{t-2,k}+ℓ_{t-1,k}`, `v_t ∝ exp(-η2 γ L_{t-1,k})`) and Theorem 2's loss all match.
5. **Cache-producing runs.** `MiceV1.step` line 222 returns one hedge over all experts from discounted log-losses (`eta=2.0, gamma=0.5` defaults, run.py:123, 216–222) — a genuinely different single-level mixture. Statement is **true**.
6. **Reviewer items.** Item 1 (init/median/merge identity) and item 2 (cache of default+fast, index notation, mean half Brier, "two adaptive prediction sequences" under `η2 ≤ 1/2`) are addressed; the "still unsupported" note on log-loss / η2=10 is now explicitly excluded in Timing. ## Findings
**F1 — P2 · Confirmed issue**
- **Location:** main.tex Algorithm 1 step 5 ("cache $\hat p_{t,k}$, $\hat p^{\mathrm{fast}}_t$, $\hat p^{\mathrm{def}}_t$") vs run.py:221 and reweight.py:124.
- **Evidence:** The released cache stores only per-expert predictions (`dict(t=t, P=dict(preds), disc={})`); `simulate2` reconstructs the fast mixture `inner` from `st["P"]` and takes `dflt = st["P"][-1000]`. The reconstruction is behaviour-preserving (weights of batch t use only labels of batches < t: `prev` and `last` are set at step t-1), which Timing already states ("replay … over the cached expert predictions", "labels of earlier batches only").
- **Why it matters:** The reviewer's demand (plan-check item 2) was that the frozen record contain the fast mixture *with its batch's original weights*; as released it is re-formed, and the paper's algorithm and its own provenance sentence describe two different cache contents.
- **Recommended correction:** In step 5 or Timing, say the cache stores the per-expert predictions ($\hat p^{\mathrm{def}}_t$ is the cached prediction of expert $M$) and that $\hat p^{\mathrm{fast}}_t$ is reconstructed from them with the weights of batch $t$, which are a function of earlier labels only — or store $\hat p^{\mathrm{fast}}_t$ in the cache.
**F2 — P2 · Missing information**
- **Location:** main.tex §4 "Experts" ($\widehat{\Div}_T(S\,|\,\cC_k)=\hat\epsilon_S(\cC_k)-\hat\epsilon_S(S)$) and Algorithm 1 step 3.
- **Evidence:** run.py `_merge_or_add` (lines 134–142) estimates both $\hat\epsilon_S(\cC_k)$ and the cross-fitted $\hat\epsilon_S(S)$ as **0-1 risk** (`(argmax == y).mean()`), never log-loss; main.tex defines $\epsilon_P(\cC)$ with a generic $\ell$ (§3 Notation) and never pins the loss of the merge statistic, while prop:blind mixes log-loss (i–ii) and 0-1 (iii).
- **Why it matters:** $\delta=0.02$ is on the scale of the chosen loss; a log-loss implementation of the same sentence merges differently and does not reproduce the pool.
- **Recommended correction:** State that $\hat\epsilon$ in the merge test is the 0-1 risk (accuracy), consistent with prop:blind(iii).
**F3 — P2 · Missing information**
- **Location:** main.tex Timing, last sentence ("All reported numbers … (simulate2 in the released code)").
- **Evidence:** `simulate2`'s defaults are `outer="log", scale=10.0, temper=False` (reweight.py:104); the described method requires `outer='brier', scale=0.5, temper=True` (with `e2=1, g2=0.9`), equivalent to the paper's $\ell$ = half Brier and $\eta_2=e\cdot\mathrm{scale}=0.5$. The paper states the mathematical parameters but not this invocation, so the frozen replay cannot be identified from the paper alone (plan-check item 3: "identify the frozen implementation/replay version"). Also, "all reported numbers … this two-level rule" coexists with separately reported variants at the log-loss and $\eta_2=10$ (Timing, §5.3) — those are replays of the same rule with variant parameters, not of "this" ($\eta_2=\tfrac12$) rule.
- **Why it matters:** Provenance/auditability of exactly which call produced the tables.
- **Recommended correction:** Name the invocation and freeze version (e.g. "`simulate2(outer='brier', scale=0.5, temper=True)`, frozen 2026-10-05") and reword to "all reported numbers come from replays of this two-level rule (registered step $\eta_2=\tfrac12$, plus the stated variants with the log-loss or $\eta_2=10$)".
