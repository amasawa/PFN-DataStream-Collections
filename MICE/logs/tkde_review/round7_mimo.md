**2. P2 | Confirmed issue | `main.tex:248–249` (remark `rem:merge`): "so merging two contexts when it is at most $\delta$ merges exactly when pooling helps more than the concepts interfere."**
- **Evidence:** Two imprecisions. (a) From `def:excess` and the preceding sentence ("empirical excess is interference minus pooling gain"), excess $\le\delta$ is equivalent to pooling gain $\ge$ interference $-\delta$; the stated equivalence "exactly when pooling helps **more than** the concepts interfere" holds only for $\delta=0$ and a non-strict comparison. The method merges at $\delta=0.02$ (`main.tex:398–399`). (b) The antecedent of "it" is the empirical excess, but the merge rule the method actually runs is on the directed form $\widehat{\Div}_T(S\,|\,\cC_k)\le\delta$ (`main.tex:397–399`); for that statistic the pooling/interference reading does not apply — $\widehat{\Div}_T\le\delta$ bounds the recall regret of the stored expert on the segment (`def:recur`). The following sentence ("`\method{}` uses the directed form ...") partly disambiguates, but the "exactly when" clause still attaches the wrong semantics to a $\delta$-threshold rule.
- **Why it matters:** As written, a reader can take "merges exactly when pooling helps more than the concepts interfere" as the interpretation of MICE's actual $\widehat{\Div}_T\le\delta$ criterion; that interpretation belongs to the excess form and is inexact at $\delta>0$.
- **Recommended correction:** E.g. "so a rule that pools two contexts when the empirical excess is at most $\delta$ does so when the pooling gain is at least the interference minus $\delta$ ($\delta=0$ gives the exact equivalence); \method{} instead merges on the directed form \eqref{eq:transfer}, which bounds the recall regret of the stored expert."
